INVESTMENT COMMITTEE PAPER
STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL
This paper is strictly confidential and is intended only for the Halden Group Investment Committee.

To:        Investment Committee, Halden Group
From:      Primary Commitments Team, Halden Group
Date:      19 October 2026
Subject:   Proposed commitment to Aldermoor Capital Fund VI

RECOMMENDATION

We recommend that the Committee approve a commitment of EUR 65 million to Aldermoor Capital Fund VI. Aldermoor is a European mid-market buyout manager. Fund VI will back companies in healthcare, business services, and industrial technology, in the DACH region, the Benelux, and the Nordics. The target enterprise value for a portfolio company is EUR 150 million to EUR 500 million. That is a range. The paper does not state an average, because the manager has not offered one and we have not invented one.

Fund VI plans to build a portfolio of 12 to 15 companies. The target fund size stated by the manager is EUR 1.1 billion. Our EUR 65 million commitment is the Halden ticket. First close is expected in January 2027. We would be in that close.

FUND OVERVIEW

Aldermoor Capital was formed in 2009 and has raised a sequence of mid-market funds. Fund VI continues the same strategy as Funds III, IV, and V: control investments in European mid-market companies, with a preference for businesses that sell to other businesses and for healthcare companies with repeat demand. The manager's own description of value creation is operational, centred on a small portfolio-support group that sits with management on pricing, procurement, and professionalising the finance function. We have seen that group on the two co-investments noted below.

The fund intends to make 12 to 15 investments. Equity cheques will vary inside the enterprise-value range of EUR 150 million to EUR 500 million. The manager has not published, and we do not state, a single average enterprise value. Sectors are healthcare, business services, and industrial technology. Geographies are DACH, Benelux, and the Nordics. The manager will look at a company outside those regions only as an exception, and has told us not to expect that exception to be a theme.

Fund V, the most recent, is still early. Several investments have been made. It is too young for a realised record, and we say so below rather than stretching the older funds across it.

INVESTMENT THESIS

The merits are the strategy we already know, the sectors, and the range of company size. A fund that intends to own 12 to 15 companies across healthcare, business services, and industrial technology in three European regions is the same shape as the Aldermoor funds we have held. The entry point for Halden is a primary commitment at first close, not a secondary, and not a co-investment in a named company. There is no single asset to describe. The thesis is the programme.

Thematic fit, set out below, is that this is the mid-market buyout programme Halden has backed with this manager since Fund III. The edge we are prepared to name is familiarity with how Aldermoor works, including two co-investments, not a claim that Fund VI will repeat a ranking the younger fund has not earned.

We would describe the attraction as the continuation of a known strategy inside a stated size range, in sectors the manager has actually invested in. We would not describe it as a promise of top-quartile results for Fund VI.

THEMATIC FIT

Halden's primary programme commits to a small number of mid-market managers and re-ups when the strategy is unchanged and the record of the mature funds supports staying. Aldermoor Fund VI is that case for this manager. The sectors and the geographies match the funds we already know. The enterprise-value range matches the companies we have seen Aldermoor buy. A ticket of EUR 65 million is consistent with the size of our recent primary commitments to managers at this scale.

The fit is the relationship and the strategy. It is not a new theme, and it is not a diversification into a region or a sector Halden has declined elsewhere.

OUR HISTORY WITH THE MANAGER

Halden has invested with Aldermoor since Fund III, which was raised in 2015. We committed to Fund III and to Fund IV, and we made a smaller commitment to Fund V. Alongside the funds we have co-invested twice, once beside Fund IV and once beside Fund V, in each case in a company the fund had already agreed to buy. Both co-investments are still held. Neither is a realised exit we could use as a return figure in this paper.

The people we work with have been stable. The two partners who led Fund III are still at the firm. One now chairs the investment committee. The portfolio-support group we met on the co-investments is the same group named in the Fund VI materials.

That is the history. It starts in 2015. It includes two co-investments. It does not include a claim that every Aldermoor fund has finished at the front of its peer set.

TRANSACTION OR TERMS

The instrument is a primary commitment of EUR 65 million to Aldermoor Capital Fund VI. The manager's target for the fund is EUR 1.1 billion. First close is expected in January 2027, and the commitment would be submitted for that close. Drawdowns will follow the fund's investments. This paper records the commitment amount because the Committee is being asked to approve it. It is not a drawn amount.

Terms are the manager's standard institutional terms for Fund VI. We have reviewed them against Fund V. The changes are ordinary updates of legal form. Nothing in the term comparison is a reason to decline or a point we are asking the Committee to negotiate in public. Fees, carry, and the investment period are in the legal draft and are not restated here as a selling point.

No co-investor is joined to our commitment. If a later company is offered as a co-investment, that will be a separate paper.

TRACK RECORD

Funds III and IV have delivered top-quartile net returns against the European mid-market peer set the manager uses, and which our own records do not contradict. Fund III is largely realised. Fund IV is partly realised and still holds several companies. The top-quartile statement is about those two funds.

Fund V is too young to judge. It has not had the time, or the exits, that would let anyone call it top quartile or otherwise. A sentence that said Aldermoor is consistently top quartile would be wrong, because it would drag Fund V into a ranking Fund V has not earned, and because two funds are not a promise about the sixth.

We will speak about the record only in those terms. Two mature funds, top quartile on a net basis. One young fund, not judged. No average enterprise value.

RISKS

Strategy drift is the risk we would notice first. If Fund VI starts buying companies above the EUR 500 million end of the range, or outside healthcare, business services, and industrial technology, the reason we are re-upping falls away. The manager has described the range as a target, not a hard cap. We will watch the first investments against it.

Fund V is unresolved. A weak development there would not rewrite Funds III and IV, and it would make a larger Fund VI harder to defend. We are committing while Fund V is too young to judge, with our eyes open.

Key-person risk is real and ordinary. The two partners from Fund III are still central. The firm has added principals. A departure of either senior partner in the fundraising would bring us back to the Committee before further closes.

Deployment risk sits on the other side. Twelve to fifteen companies is a plan. A slow start would leave the commitment undrawn for longer, which is a pacing question, not a reason to invent a pipeline the manager has not named. The private placement memorandum lists sectors and geographies. It does not list a set of companies already agreed.

OVERALL VIEW

The investment team considers Aldermoor Capital Fund VI to be an attractive entry into European mid-market buyout, which benefits from a relationship running since Fund III in 2015 and from two co-investments, while the structure offers a plan for 12 to 15 companies at enterprise values of EUR 150 million to EUR 500 million.
