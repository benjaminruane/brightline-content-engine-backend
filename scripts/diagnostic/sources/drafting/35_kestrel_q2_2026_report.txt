SUMMARY

Net asset value rose over the quarter, and the rise was modest. The holding that stood out was Harbourline Foods, which had a strong quarter in its chilled ranges. No company was written down by a comparable amount. The fund did not buy or sell a business in the quarter. The agreement to sell a stake in Harbourline was announced on 17 June, and at 30 June it had not completed.

At 30 June 2026 the fund's net TVPI was 1.36 times and the net IRR was 14.8 percent. Both figures are net to limited partners. They are not the same figures the third-quarter report will carry, because this report stops at June.

HARBOURLINE FOODS

Harbourline Foods produces chilled meals, pies, and side dishes for retailers in the United Kingdom and Ireland. The quarter was strong. National retailers took the summer range in the volumes the company had planned for, and the Cork plant ran close to the rate the expansion was built for. Waste on the Yorkshire lines was lower than in the first quarter. Management described the quarter as the best trading period since the prior summer, on the measures they use internally: cases shipped, service level, and the margin on the chilled range.

On 17 June 2026 the fund announced an agreement to sell a 60 percent stake to Northshore Consumer Partners, keeping 40 percent. At the date of this report the sale had not completed. Harbourline is still a controlled holding in the table below. The strong quarter is an operating result. It is not the cash from a sale. Partners should not read the modest rise in fund net asset value as if the exit had already been received. The valuation at 30 June reflects the trading and the fact that an agreement exists. Completion, if it happens on the timetable in the announcement, belongs to a later report.

We have owned Harbourline for several years. The work in those years was to make the company a supplier that national retailers could plan a season around. This quarter is evidence that the plants and the range can do that. It is one quarter.

AURELIA HEALTH

Aurelia Health's rostering software continued to be rolled out at the hospital groups already on the platform. No new group was signed in the quarter. The existing rollouts are the activity. The valuation moved up by a small amount on the progress of those rollouts. It was not the main contributor to the fund. Aurelia remains a holding we are content with, and it was a quiet quarter beside Harbourline.

BRIGHTWATER LABS

Brightwater Labs, which sells analytical software and laboratory services to testing networks, traded in line with the first quarter. The largest customer remained in place at 30 June. There is nothing in this report that anticipates a later loss of that customer, because at this date the contract stood. The valuation was held essentially flat. We record that so a reader of a later report can see that June was not the quarter of a write-down.

OTHER HOLDINGS

Three smaller software companies made up the rest of the portfolio. One released a version of its product on time. One hired a head of customer teams. One renewed an annual contract with its largest user. None of these changed the fund's net asset value in a way a partner needs a separate paragraph to understand. VALUATION AND FIGURES

The modest increase in net asset value is the number partners should hold onto. We use the word modest because the move is small. Harbourline's quarter was strong at the company. The fund's net asset value did not rise by a matching proportion, because Harbourline is one holding, because the sale had not completed, and because the other holdings were quiet.

Net TVPI of 1.36 times and net IRR of 14.8 percent are the fund-level figures at 30 June 2026. They are net. They will not be repeated unchanged if a later quarter revalues a holding or receives sale proceeds. This report does not forecast the third quarter.

CASH

Calls and distributions during the quarter were the ordinary ones under the fund agreement and were not the story of the period. The story was Harbourline's trading, the modest rise in net asset value, and an agreed sale that had not yet completed.

OUTLOOK

The third quarter, as we saw it in July, had two known events and one unknown. The known events were whether the Harbourline sale would complete on its conditions, and whether Aurelia's rollouts would continue at the same pace. The unknown was ordinary trading at the other companies. We did not, in July, have a Brightwater customer loss to report, and this document does not invent one.
