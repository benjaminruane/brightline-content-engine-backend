Subject:  Helvetia Precision Components AG - add-on acquisition of Morava Precision s.r.o.

1. RECOMMENDATION

The team recommends that the Committee approve the acquisition by Helvetia Precision Components AG ("Helvetia") of 100% of Morava Precision s.r.o. ("Morava"), a precision machining business based in Brno, Czech Republic. The share purchase agreement was signed on 3 November 2026. Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to clearance by the Czech competition authority.

Consideration is EUR 42 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis. Helvetia will fund the purchase from cash on balance sheet and an EUR 18 million accordion under its existing senior facility, for which lender consent has been obtained. No additional equity is required from the Halden Group funds.

2. MORAVA AT A GLANCE

Morava was founded in 2004 by the Novak family and machines titanium structural fittings and small rotating parts for civil aircraft programmes. The business employs around 310 people at a single site in Brno and holds AS9100 certification together with the customer approvals needed to ship flight hardware directly to its three principal customers, all European aerostructures manufacturers. These three customers account for around 80% of sales; the longest relationship dates back to 2011.

For the twelve months to June 2026, Morava generated revenue of EUR 64 million and EBITDA of EUR 9 million (14% margin). Titanium scrap rates have improved materially over the past three years and are now close to Helvetia's own rates on stainless steel work. Current lead times are around 14 weeks, compared with more than 20 weeks at Helvetia's Swiss aerospace cell.

3. INVESTMENT RATIONALE

Capacity where the platform needs it. Helvetia's aerospace order book has outgrown its Swiss titanium capacity, and the business currently meets demand through overtime at Winterthur and Olten and through three external machining suppliers, one of which is Morava. Morava's large-format titanium milling cells are exactly the capability the Swiss sites lack, and labour and energy costs in Brno are substantially below those in Switzerland. Management plans to load the Brno cells with Helvetia overflow work within 18 months of completion while retaining Morava's existing customer base.

A known asset. Helvetia has bought titanium fittings from Morava for two civil aircraft programmes since 2019, so its quality, delivery and engineering record are known first-hand rather than through diligence alone. This reduces integration and quality risk relative to a typical add-on.

Operational adjacency. Helvetia has operated a smaller parts plant in Brno since 2021. Supervision, quality management and procurement can be shared across the two sites from day one.

The case does not rely on multiple arbitrage. Value creation comes from moving work out of high-cost Swiss overtime and third-party suppliers onto owned, lower-cost capacity.

4. FIT WITH THE HELVETIA INVESTMENT THESIS

When Halden Group acquired Helvetia in 2025, the investment thesis included a buy-and-build programme of two to three machining add-ons over the holding period, focused on capacity that could not be added cost-effectively in Switzerland. Morava is the first of these and sits squarely in the aerospace leg of the plan. It deepens Helvetia's position in an existing end market rather than extending the platform into a new one.

5. HISTORY WITH THE ASSET

Beyond the supplier relationship since 2019, Helvetia completed a full vendor audit of Morava in 2024. Halden Group's operating team supported Helvetia management through confirmatory diligence and joined two site visits to Brno in 2026, which confirmed cell layout, certifications and customer approvals and identified no quality or customer issues.

6. TRANSACTION STRUCTURE

The sellers are the Novak family and a small group of employee shareholders. A customary escrow will be held against warranty claims, and the founders will provide transition support for six months, after which they will not hold operating roles. Morava's management will report to Helvetia's aerospace director from completion. Halden Group's ownership of Helvetia is unchanged and no co-investors are involved.

7. KEY RISKS

Customer concentration: three customers account for around 80% of sales. Two have provided change-of-control comfort letters; the third is expected to sign before completion.
Titanium price exposure: alloy costs are largely passed through to customers, but with a lag.
Integration: alignment of quality systems and management of a second Czech site.
Clearance timing: a delay beyond the first quarter of 2027 would defer the capacity benefit but would not affect the transaction terms.

8. CONCLUSION

The investment team considers Morava an attractive way to add low-cost, aerospace-qualified titanium capacity to Helvetia at a point when the Swiss sites are capacity-constrained, with execution risk reduced by a five-year supply relationship and an existing Helvetia presence in Brno, and funded entirely within the Helvetia capital structure.
