Subject:   Helvetia Precision Components AG, proposed acquisition of Morava Precision s.r.o.

RECOMMENDATION

We ask the Committee to note, and to approve at Helvetia level, the acquisition by Helvetia Precision Components AG of Morava Precision s.r.o., a Brno machining business. Helvetia is the buyer. Halden Group is not investing new equity. The purchase will be funded from Helvetia's own cash flow and from an extended debt facility at Helvetia. The agreement was signed on 3 November 2026. Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to Czech merger clearance, which counsel expects to be routine.

The consideration is EUR 42 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis. Helvetia will draw EUR 18 million from an extension of its existing senior facility and will fund the balance from cash already on its balance sheet. No draw is requested from Halden Group, and the holding company's ownership of Helvetia is unchanged.

COMPANY OVERVIEW

Morava Precision s.r.o. machines aerospace titanium parts in Brno. The business was founded in 2004 by the Novak family and employs about 310 people on a single site. Revenue for the year to June 2026 was EUR 64 million, with EBITDA of EUR 9 million, a margin of about 14 percent. The work is concentrated in structural fittings and small rotating parts for civil aircraft programmes. The customer list is short and durable: three European aerostructures companies account for about four fifths of sales, and the longest of those relationships began in 2011.

The plant runs large titanium milling cells that Helvetia's own Swiss sites do not have in the same number. Labour and energy costs in Brno are materially below Winterthur and Olten. Lead times on the current book are about fourteen weeks, against more than twenty weeks at Helvetia's Swiss aerospace cell. Scrap on titanium has been brought down over three years and now sits close to the rate Helvetia achieves on stainless work.

Morava is not a medical or semiconductor supplier. Its equipment, its quality system, and its customer approvals are aerospace. That is the capacity Helvetia wants. The site holds AS9100 and the customer approvals that let it ship flight hardware without a fresh qualification for the three main accounts.

INVESTMENT THESIS

Buy-and-build is the stated theme for Helvetia. The original investment memorandum set out a plan to add two or three machining businesses over the hold, aimed at capacity that the Swiss footprint cannot add cheaply. Morava is the first of those additions. It gives Helvetia a low-cost titanium cell already qualified for aerospace, in a city where Helvetia has operated a smaller parts plant since 2021.

The industrial case is straightforward. Helvetia currently buys titanium fittings from Morava and from two other outside shops. Bringing Morava inside lets Helvetia move a defined book of aerospace work from Swiss overtime and from third parties onto a plant that was built for that alloy. Management's plan, which we have reviewed but not re-underwritten in this paper, is to fill the Brno cells with Helvetia overflow within eighteen months of completion, while keeping Morava's existing aerospace customers.

We are not relying on a multiple re-rating. The case is capacity, cost, and a customer overlap we already know because Helvetia has been on the other side of the supply relationship.

THEMATIC FIT

Helvetia was acquired as a precision-components platform serving medical devices, aerospace, and semiconductor equipment. The add-on programme was part of that thesis, not a later invention. Morava sits in the aerospace leg of that plan. It does not extend Helvetia into a new end market, and it does not require Halden to take a new direct position. It deepens a platform we already own, in the geography and the process the original plan named.

The Brno site also sits beside Helvetia's existing Czech plant. Supervision, quality staff, and a shared procurement desk are practical, not theoretical. The thematic point is the platform strategy: one more qualified aerospace cell, bought by the company that will use it.

OUR HISTORY WITH THE ASSET

Helvetia has bought from Morava as a supplier since 2019. The purchases were titanium fittings for two civil programmes. Volumes have been steady rather than large, and the quality record over that period is the reason Helvetia put Morava on the short list. Halden Group has had no direct holding in Morava. Our knowledge of the business is through Helvetia's purchasing records, a vendor audit in 2024, and the diligence now completed by Helvetia's management with our operating team in support.

We visited the Brno site twice this year with Helvetia's head of aerospace. The visits confirmed the cell layout and the customer approvals. They did not surface a customer dispute or a quality escape that would change the recommendation.

TRANSACTION OR TERMS

Helvetia Precision Components AG is the acquiring entity. The sellers are the Novak family and a small employee pool. They will leave a customary escrow for warranty claims and will provide transition support for six months. No Halden equity is being invested. The EUR 42 million consideration is met from Helvetia's cash and from the EUR 18 million extension of Helvetia's own debt facility. Helvetia's lenders have consented to the extension. The facility remains at Helvetia. It is not a Halden obligation and it is not new equity.

Signing took place on 3 November 2026. Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, once Czech merger clearance is received. Helvetia's ownership by Halden is unchanged by the transaction. There is no co-investor and no new holding-company instrument.

Management of Morava will report to Helvetia's aerospace director from completion. The Novak family will not retain an operating role past the transition period.

RISKS

Customer concentration at Morava is real. Three accounts dominate sales. Helvetia's plan depends on those accounts staying through the change of control, which the contracts allow them to accept or to discuss. Two of the three have given comfort letters. The third has not refused, and has not yet signed.

Titanium prices move. Morava passes a large part of alloy cost through to customers, but the pass-through is lagged. A sharp move between signing and completion would sit with Helvetia. We have not treated that as a reason to delay, because the same exposure already exists in the supply relationship.

Integration risk is the ordinary one: a newly acquired plant, a different language on the shop floor, and a quality system that must be brought onto Helvetia's. The existing supply relationship reduces the unknown. It does not remove the work.

Clearance risk is low on the advice we have. A delay past the first quarter of 2027 would push the capacity benefit by a quarter. It would not change who is buying or how the purchase is funded.

OVERALL VIEW

The investment team considers Morava Precision to be an attractive entry into low-cost aerospace titanium capacity, which benefits from Helvetia's supply relationship since 2019, while the structure offers funding entirely from Helvetia's own cash flow and debt, with no new equity from Halden Group.
