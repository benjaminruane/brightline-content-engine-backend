INVESTMENT COMMITTEE PAPER
STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL
This paper is strictly confidential and is intended only for the Halden Group Investment Committee.

To:        Investment Committee, Halden Group
From:      Secondaries Team, Halden Group
Date:      8 September 2026
Subject:   Proposed purchase of five LP interests from Vestmark Pension Scheme

RECOMMENDATION

We recommend that the Committee approve the purchase from Vestmark Pension Scheme of limited-partner interests in five buyout funds, managed by four firms. The interests are mainly in European and North American mid-market buyouts, with vintages from 2016 to 2021. The price is a discount of about one eighth to reported net asset value. We are not asking the Committee to treat this as a purchase of a mature portfolio, and we are not an existing investor in all five funds.

The seller is a pension scheme reducing the number of manager relationships. The process was a negotiated sale of a named list, not an auction of a whole book. Closing is targeted for November 2026, subject to general-partner consents, which have been requested and not refused.

FUND OVERVIEW

The five funds, and the position Halden already has with each, are these.

Northlane Buyout III, vintage 2016, Northlane Capital. A European mid-market fund, now harvesting. Halden is an existing investor.

Cinderwell Partners II, vintage 2017, Cinderwell. A North American mid-market fund, mostly harvesting, with two companies still held for a later exit. Halden is an existing investor.

Hartham Equity IV, vintage 2018, Hartham. A European mid-market fund, harvesting. Halden is not an investor. We diligenced Hartham on an earlier secondary in 2022 and did not proceed, for price, not for the manager.

Northlane Buyout IV, vintage 2019, Northlane Capital. The successor to the 2016 fund, mostly harvesting, with a small tail of newer investments. Halden is an existing investor. This is the third fund of the five in which we already hold an interest.

Pellam Mid-Market I, vintage 2021, Pellam. A European mid-market fund that is still in its investment period. Halden is not an investor, and this is not the fund we diligenced in 2022. One of the five is still investing. The other four are mostly harvesting.

Four managers, five funds. Three existing holdings. One earlier diligence. One fund we have not owned and did not diligence on that earlier trade. Sectors across the book are business services, industrial technology, healthcare, and a smaller consumer sleeve in the Cinderwell fund. Geographies are western Europe and North America. The 2016 to 2019 funds are in harvest. The 2021 fund is not.

INVESTMENT THESIS

The merits are the price, the overlap with managers we already know, and a book that is mostly through its investment period without being finished. A discount of about one eighth to reported net asset value is the pricing. We state it in words because the papers do not rest on a single percentage that all five general partners would compute the same way. Reported net asset value is the reference. It is not a promise of exit value.

The 2021 fund is the part of the book that can still call capital and can still make new investments. That is a feature of a mixed-vintage purchase, and it is the reason a reader would be wrong to call the portfolio mature. We underwrite the four harvesting funds on the companies they already hold, and we underwrite Pellam on the capital that can still be drawn and on the investments already made.

We are not underwriting a turnaround of a broken fund. The reports we have read describe ordinary buyout portfolios at this age: some companies ready for sale, some that will take longer, and one fund that is still deploying.

THEMATIC FIT

Halden's secondaries programme buys limited-partner interests in mid-market buyout funds where we already have, or have recently formed, a view of the manager. This list fits that programme. It is not a single-asset restructuring and it is not a primary commitment. The thematic point is a diversified set of mid-market interests, priced below reported value, with a known overlap on three of the five.

The programme's preference for managers we can reference is met on Northlane and Cinderwell by our existing accounts, and on Hartham by the 2022 diligence. Pellam is the name on this list that the programme has not previously studied at the same depth. The paper does not pretend otherwise. The Pellam work for this trade is new.

OUR HISTORY WITH THE ASSET

Halden is an existing investor in three of the five funds: Northlane Buyout III, Cinderwell Partners II, and Northlane Buyout IV. We diligenced a fourth, Hartham Equity IV, in an earlier secondary in 2022 and walked away on price. We have no existing interest in Hartham Equity IV and no existing interest in Pellam Mid-Market I.

That history is the whole of the overlap. It is three funds, not five. The two Northlane funds are the same manager at two vintages, which is why four managers cover five funds. Our Northlane relationship is the longest of the three. Cinderwell is an account we have held since that fund's first close. Hartham is a file we reopened, not a holding. Pellam is new to us.

TRANSACTION OR TERMS

The seller is Vestmark Pension Scheme. The buyer would be Halden's secondaries vehicle. The price is a discount of about one eighth to the net asset value reported by the five general partners at 30 June 2026, with a customary true-up when the September statements arrive. General-partner consent is required for each transfer. Consents have been requested. None has been refused at the date of this paper. Completion is targeted for November 2026.

Unfunded commitments travel with the interests. They are largest, by a wide margin, on the 2021 Pellam fund, which is still investing. The harvesting funds have smaller residual obligations. The purchase agreement sets the treatment of those obligations in the ordinary way. This paper does not restate the cash figures. The point for the Committee is that future funding is concentrated on the one fund that has not finished investing.

No co-buyer is proposed. The seller will transfer the whole of each named interest.

RISKS

Valuation risk sits in the reported net asset values. A discount of about one eighth is only a discount if those values are close to what a buyer of the companies would pay. Two of the portfolios have a company the manager has held longer than the original plan. We have read the explanations. They are plausible. They are not exits.

Consent risk is mechanical and can delay November. A refused consent on one fund would force a decision on whether the other four are still the trade. We would come back to the Committee rather than drop a fund in silence.

The 2021 fund can still make a poor new investment after we have bought the interest. That risk is the price of including a fund that is not yet harvesting. Calling the book mature would hide it.

Manager concentration is moderate. Northlane is two of the five funds. A problem at that manager would touch two interests. We already live with Northlane as an existing investor, so this purchase adds exposure we have chosen before, at a discount to the reported value.

Pellam is the name we know least. The diligence on that fund is the thinnest of the five, because we had no prior file. We have read the last four quarterly reports and spoken to the investor-relations contact. We have not visited a portfolio company.

OVERALL VIEW

The investment team considers the Vestmark interests to be an attractive entry into a mainly harvesting mid-market book, which benefits from an existing holding in three of the five funds, while the structure offers a discount of about one eighth to reported net asset value and leaves one 2021 fund still investing.
