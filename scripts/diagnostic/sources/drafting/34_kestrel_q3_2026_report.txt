SUMMARY

Net asset value declined by about 2 percent over the quarter. The decline was mainly a write-down of Brightwater Labs after the company lost a major customer. Gains at Aurelia Health offset part of that write-down and were not large enough to reverse it. The fund also completed the acquisition of Fjordline Software and the sale of a 60 percent stake in Harbourline Foods. A further investment, Selkirk Analytics, is in exclusivity and is not yet a portfolio company.

At 30 September 2026 the fund's net TVPI was 1.29 times and the net IRR was 13.1 percent. Both figures are net to limited partners. They are lower than the figures in the second-quarter report. The change is the Brightwater write-down, partly offset by Aurelia and by the Harbourline sale.

BRIGHTWATER LABS

Brightwater Labs sells analytical software and related laboratory services to a small number of large customers in water testing and food safety. During the quarter its largest customer, a national testing network, ended the contract. The customer had been a little over a third of Brightwater's revenue. The loss was not a dispute about quality. The customer took the work back into its own laboratories after a change of ownership at the customer.

We wrote the holding down to reflect the revenue that has gone and the time it will take to replace it. The write-down is the main reason the fund's net asset value fell. Management is cutting cost in line with the smaller book and is in talks with two smaller networks. Neither talk is a signed contract. We do not treat them as a recovery. The valuation now assumes a smaller company than the one we marked at 30 June.

AURELIA HEALTH

Aurelia Health provides software that hospital groups use to roster clinical staff. The quarter was the other side of the fund's result. Two hospital groups expanded the number of sites on the system, and the Danish bolt-on that the October capital call later funded was agreed in principle during September. The gain in Aurelia's valuation offset part of the Brightwater write-down. It did not offset all of it. Aurelia is performing in line with the plan we described when the fund first invested. The offset is real and partial.

FJORDLINE SOFTWARE

In October, just after the quarter-end for signing and within the events this report covers for partners, Fund IV acquired Fjordline Software. Fjordline, based in Oslo, sells software that freight operators use to plan loads, yards, and delivery slots. Customers are logistics companies in the Nordic region and northern Germany. The acquisition closed in the first days of the fourth quarter. We include it in this report because partners will see it in the capital account that accompanies these pages, and because the call that funded it was noticed in October against work that was agreed in September.

Fjordline is a new company in the portfolio. It is not a contributor to the 2 percent decline. It had not been marked for a full quarter.

HARBOURLINE FOODS

On 30 September 2026 the fund completed the sale of a 60 percent stake in Harbourline Foods. The fund retains 40 percent. The agreement had been announced on 17 June 2026. Completion is the event this quarter records. Harbourline produces chilled foods for retailers in the United Kingdom and Ireland. The partial sale is a realisation of the majority of the position and a decision to remain a minority owner alongside Northshore Consumer Partners. The retained 40 percent is now carried as a minority holding. The sold stake is the source of the distribution partners received in October.

SELKIRK ANALYTICS

Selkirk Analytics is in exclusivity. It is a data-services business that cleans and routes operational data for mid-sized manufacturers. We have agreed exclusivity with the seller. We have not signed a purchase agreement, and Selkirk is not in the portfolio table below. We mention it so partners are not surprised if a later report shows a new company, and so nobody reads this quarter as if the investment were already made. Exclusivity can end without a deal.

OTHER MOVEMENTS

The three smaller software holdings were revalued in the ordinary way. None of them moved enough to explain the quarter. One renewed a contract with an existing customer. One slipped a product release by a few weeks. We have not changed the narrative of the fund on either of those.

Cash at the fund rose with the Harbourline proceeds and then fell with the distribution and with the funding of Fjordline. The net asset value movement partners should remember is the 2 percent decline, caused mainly by Brightwater, partly offset by Aurelia.

OUTLOOK

The fourth quarter opens with Fjordline inside the fund, Harbourline as a retained minority, and Brightwater smaller than it was in June. Selkirk either becomes an investment or the exclusivity lapses. We will report which, when we know. We are not guiding a recovery of the Brightwater revenue in this quarter's numbers.

Net TVPI of 1.29 times and net IRR of 13.1 percent are the fund-level figures at 30 September 2026. They replace the second-quarter figures. They are not a forecast.
