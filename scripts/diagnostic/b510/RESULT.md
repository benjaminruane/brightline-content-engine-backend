Dear valued investors,

This letter provides an update on Helvetia Precision Components AG and its buy-and-build strategy, focusing on the signed acquisition of Morava Precision s.r.o. as an add-on. Helvetia is the acquirer. The transaction is funded entirely at the portfolio company level and does not involve new equity from Halden Group.

Helvetia was acquired as a precision-components platform serving medical devices, aerospace, and semiconductor equipment, with a stated buy-and-build strategy. The original plan anticipated adding 2 to 3 machining businesses over the holding period, focused on capacity that Helvetia’s Swiss footprint could not add cost-effectively. Morava is the first of these planned additions and sits squarely in the aerospace leg of the strategy. It adds qualified aerospace machining capacity in a geography and process that were identified in the original investment thesis.

Morava Precision is a Brno-based machining business that produces aerospace titanium parts. Founded in 2004 by the Novak family, it employs about 310 people on a single site. For the year to June 2026, Morava generated revenue of EUR 64 million and EBITDA of EUR 9 million, an EBITDA margin of about 14%. Its work is concentrated in structural fittings and small rotating parts for civil aircraft programs, with 3 European aerostructures companies accounting for about four fifths of sales, including one relationship that dates back to 2011.

The target adds capabilities and capacity that Helvetia does not have in the same form in Switzerland. Morava operates large titanium milling cells that Helvetia’s Swiss sites in Winterthur and Olten do not have in comparable numbers. Labor and energy costs in Brno are materially below those in Switzerland, and Morava’s lead times on its current book are about 14 weeks, compared with more than 20 weeks at Helvetia’s Swiss aerospace cell. Over the last 3 years, Morava has reduced titanium scrap to a level close to Helvetia’s rate on stainless work. The Brno site holds AS9100 certification and customer approvals that allow it to ship flight hardware to its 3 main accounts without fresh qualification.

Strategically, Morava provides Helvetia with a low-cost, aerospace-qualified titanium cell in a city where Helvetia has operated a smaller parts plant since 2021. Helvetia already buys titanium fittings from Morava and from two other external shops. By bringing Morava in-house, Helvetia expects to move a defined book of aerospace work away from Swiss overtime and third-party suppliers onto a plant built for titanium machining. Management’s plan, which the investment team has reviewed, is to fill the Brno cells with Helvetia overflow within 18 months of completion while retaining Morava’s existing aerospace customers. The investment team is not relying on a multiple re-rating; the case rests on capacity, cost, and a customer overlap that Helvetia already knows from its existing supply relationship.

The rationale for proceeding with this add-on now is tied to both Helvetia’s performance and the platform strategy. The add-on program was part of the original Helvetia thesis, not a later change of direction. Morava deepens Helvetia’s aerospace footprint without extending into a new end market and without requiring additional equity from Halden Group. The Brno site is adjacent to Helvetia’s existing Czech plant, which should allow practical sharing of supervision, quality staff, and procurement. In the investment team’s view, this is a logical step in executing the platform’s buy-and-build plan using the company’s own balance sheet and debt capacity.

Helvetia has a multi-year history with Morava as a supplier. Since 2019, Helvetia has purchased titanium fittings from Morava for two civil aircraft programs. Volumes have been steady rather than large, and Morava’s quality record over this period was a key reason it was shortlisted as an acquisition candidate. Halden Group has not previously held a direct stake in Morava; knowledge of the business has come through Helvetia’s purchasing records, a vendor audit in 2024, and the diligence now completed by Helvetia’s management with support from Halden’s operating team. Site visits to Brno in 2026 with Helvetia’s head of aerospace confirmed the cell layout and customer approvals and did not identify customer disputes or quality issues that would alter the recommendation.

The transaction was signed on 3 November 2026. Helvetia will acquire Morava Precision s.r.o. from the Novak family and a small employee pool for EUR 42 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis. The sellers will leave a customary escrow for warranty claims and will provide transition support for 6 months. Helvetia will fund the purchase with an EUR 18 million extension of its existing senior facility and cash already on its balance sheet. Helvetia’s lenders have consented to the extension. The facility remains at Helvetia and is not a Halden obligation. There is no change to the holding company’s ownership of Helvetia, no co-investor, and no new holding-company instrument.

Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to Czech merger clearance, which counsel expects to be routine. Management of Morava will report to Helvetia’s aerospace director after closing, and the Novak family will not retain an operating role beyond the transition period. The investment team notes several risks: customer concentration at Morava, with 3 accounts dominating sales; exposure to titanium price movements, where pass-through to customers is lagged; and standard integration risks related to a new plant, a different shop-floor language, and alignment of quality systems. Two of the 3 key customers have provided comfort letters regarding the change of control, while the third has neither refused nor yet signed.

In the investment team’s overall view, Morava Precision represents an entry into low-cost aerospace titanium capacity that aligns with Helvetia’s original buy-and-build plan. The team believes the existing supply relationship since 2019, the operational fit with Helvetia’s aerospace business, and the ability to fund the acquisition entirely from Helvetia’s cash flow and debt support the decision to proceed without new equity from Halden Group.

The firm increased the portfolio company's fair value by 14% for the quarter ended 30 September 2026, relative to 30 June 2026. The valuation methodology is unchanged, based on an EV/EBITDA multiple derived from the established trading peer set, with equity value calculated after deducting net debt.

The firm views these multiple and currency effects as external to the underlying factory performance and has recommended that the mark be recorded on that basis.

Operationally, the portfolio company continued to perform in line with the original investment thesis. Medical demand was solid, with orthopedic customers maintaining first-half order patterns and the largest medical account continuing to ship against a product launch that began in the prior year. Aerospace volumes were steady. The semiconductor segment was soft, as 2 equipment customers deferred tool orders, resulting in lower semiconductor sales than in the June quarter and below budget for the period.

EBITDA increased quarter-on-quarter, supported by a more favorable mix as higher-margin medical work represented a larger share of volumes than semiconductor work. Procurement savings continued at a modest pace against the year-one plan but were not the main driver of the valuation change. Net debt moved only with ordinary cash generation and a small working-capital swing, and there were no changes to the capital structure, ownership, or governance.

The firm continues to work with management against the original plan to widen margins through footprint optimization, procurement, and automation. Regular engagement in the quarter consisted of monthly calls with the chief executive and a site day at Olten; no additional diligence was commissioned for this valuation. The investment thesis remains centered on a defensive medical and aerospace franchise, with a smaller, cyclical semiconductor exposure that has previously shown similar soft patches, notably in 2023 before the firm's entry.

Looking ahead, the firm notes several near-term risks. If semiconductor equipment orders do not recover in the fourth quarter, EBITDA may not repeat the current quarter's level, and the higher peer multiple could then sit on a weaker earnings base, potentially reversing part of this quarter's uplift in future marks. Currency could also move against the portfolio company; a weaker Swiss franc would unwind part of the current valuation increase without any change in operating performance. Peer multiples may de-rate, which would affect the valuation even if medical demand remains solid. The firm therefore regards the revised fair value as a measured mark that reflects both the underlying resilience in medical and aerospace and the externally driven impact of higher market multiples and currency.
